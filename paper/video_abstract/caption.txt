Patient-specific ECG morphology motivates on-device personalization. A frozen 1-D CNN with 1,314 parameters maps each already segmented beat to a 32-value embedding. A few labeled patient beats define a mean embedding, or prototype, for each class; new beats are assigned to the nearest prototype. Adaptation requires no gradient update. On eligible held-out MIT-BIH DS2 patients, mean per-patient macro-F1 rises from 0.635/0.639/0.646 to 0.731/0.771/0.797 with 1/5/10 support beats per class. In a restricted mode, updating only the normal prototype gives a paired absolute gain of 0.043–0.052 macro-F1 without patient-specific arrhythmia annotations; both modes use identical query beats within each shot count. The experiment uses reference annotations to identify normal support. On a PSoC 6 Cortex-M4F, classification takes 11.39 ms per already segmented beat; upstream detection is excluded. The 5.2 KB flash and 22.2 KB SRAM figures are model-centric, not the full firmware footprint. The video shows MIT-BIH beats and an illustrative patient embedding, plus aggregate DS2 results.
